Closed-loop compression

We encode, measure the real bytes, and retry at adjusted settings until your image fits 200KB or cannot go smaller at a usable size. No guessing, no fake success messages.

Output is JPG

Upload forms with size limits want JPG, so that is what you get, at the highest quality that fits the byte budget. Transparent areas become white.

Never a bigger file

If your image is already at or under the target, or recompression would grow it, you get your original back unchanged with a clear message.

Frequently asked questions

Can every image reach 200KB?

Most photos can. Extremely detailed images may bottom out slightly above very small targets at usable dimensions. When that happens we return the smallest usable result and say so plainly, instead of pretending it worked.

What quality will I get?

The best quality that fits the byte budget. We search for the highest JPG quality that lands under 200KB, downscaling dimensions only when necessary (output is capped at 2400 pixels wide; check the destination's required dimensions and format).

Which input formats work?

JPG, PNG, WebP, and iPhone HEIC photos. Whatever you upload, the result comes back as a JPG under the target when reachable.

Does compressing change my original file?

Never. Your original stays untouched on your device. The tool sends back a new, smaller copy as a download, so a bad result costs you nothing.

Why is my compressed image exactly a JPG?

Because upload forms with KB limits expect JPG. PNG rarely fits tight byte budgets for photos, so we always deliver JPG at the highest quality that fits 200KB.
